# hasawebsite: The concept

## 1. Where the idea came from

I recently built my personal portfolio website at [umroo.dev](https://umroo.dev). During the process, I realized how difficult building a personal website can be for a developer who doesn't know much about design.

I've also noticed a common complaint on social media:

> "Building a personal website is harder than creating a website for someone else."

While designing my portfolio, I realized that its social media-inspired layout could be generalized. With a few tweaks and customization options, almost anyone could use the same general structure to create a personal website that reflects their own personality.

My portfolio looks like a mix of a Discord, LinkedIn, and Twitter profile. This became the starting point for the idea.

## 2. The idea

Create a simple, component-driven website builder that uses the general layout of my portfolio as its foundation.

The app would let users create their own personal websites by choosing, adding, arranging, and customizing predefined components.

The goal is to make building a personal website as simple as possible without forcing users into the complicated process of designing and iterating on a website from scratch.

### Taking inspiration from Linktree

I want the app to be much simpler than a traditional website builder, taking inspiration from the simplicity of Linktree.

However, Linktree doesn't fully meet the needs of developers who want a personal website where they can display their skills, projects, experience, and personality in a professional way.

My idea combines:

- The simplicity of creating a Linktree page.
- The professional information and structure of a personal portfolio.
- The familiar design of social media profiles.
- The freedom to customize and express your own personality.

## 3. The website format

Like Linktree, users would get their own website without needing to purchase or configure a domain.

However, instead of the traditional `linktr.ee/username` format, I want to use personalized subdomains.

For example:

```
username.hasaweb.site
```

Which translates to:

```
umroo.hasaweb.site
```

Or, in plain English:

"Umroo has a website."

I find this an appealing and playful way for someone to own and express their personal website.

## 4. The website builder

The editor should be simple enough for anyone to understand and use without prior experience in web design.

### Editor layout

The editor would have two main sections:

- **Customization and components panel:** A side panel where users can select components, add content, and customize their website.
- **Live website preview:** The main area where users build their website and immediately see how the published version will look.

### Component-driven approach

Instead of having to design individual sections, users would build their websites using predefined components.

Users should be able to:

- Add components from a component picker.
- Arrange and reorder components through drag and drop.
- Customize the content and appearance of each component.
- See their changes immediately in the live preview.

The system should also allow new components to be introduced over time.

## 5. Website components

The builder would provide a collection of ready-made components that users can add depending on what they want to display.

Some examples include:

- GitHub activity graphs
- Skills displays
- Project displays
- Work experience sections
- About me sections
- Contact forms
- Social media links
- Resume displays

The possibilities are endless, and the component library can continue to grow with new options.

## 6. Customization and personal expression

One of the most important aspects of the app is allowing users to make their websites feel like their own.

Although every website would follow the same general social media profile format, users should have enough freedom to express their personalities.

### Personal information

Like my existing portfolio, users could add:

- A profile picture and banner
- Their personal bio
- Social media profiles
- Their resume
- Their skills, projects, and experience

### Visual customization

One of my favorite details from my portfolio is the Discord-inspired avatar decoration that overlays the profile picture.

Small details like this can add personality to an otherwise professional-looking website.

I want users to have access to similar customization options so that their websites don't all look identical.

### The balance

The challenge is finding the right balance between simplicity and customization.

The app should:

- Follow a consistent social media-inspired layout.
- Give users enough customization to make their websites feel personal.
- Avoid the complexity of traditional website builders.
- Maintain a polished, visually appealing design regardless of the components users choose.

The final website should still feel like something the user can call their own.

## 7. The motto and purpose

### "Everyone should have a website."

Building a personal website shouldn't require going through a complicated design and iteration process.

The name "hasawebsite" comes from this idea. Everyone should be able to say that they have their own website.

### "All your social profiles merged into one."

The social media-inspired design is another central part of the concept.

Instead of having information scattered across different platforms, users could have one personal website that brings together their identity, professional information, projects, and social profiles.

## 8. The end goal

Build a website builder that is simple to use, visually appealing, polished, and full of personality.

The experience should be straightforward enough that users can create their websites without worrying about design decisions or complicated configuration.

At the same time, the customization options should be flexible enough that each website feels personal, even though they all share the same general format.

**The ultimate goal is for someone to create, customize, and publish their own personal website on a subdomain in under 15 minutes.**
